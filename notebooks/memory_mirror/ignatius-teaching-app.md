## Essay 1 — Why do you want to work at Saint Ignatius College Prep?
> I have a strong passion for intellectual growth, enjoy inculcating that passion within others, and want to do this as part of a larger path toward growing spiritually in service to others. With a PhD in physics, I bring a high caliber of intellectual rigor—a caliber that I believe will harmonize with St. Ignatius’ well-known, high academic standards. One friend of mine who works at St. Ignatius, Christine Mayeaux, speaks highly of the students, their character, and their desire to learn and grow; it would be a pleasure to teach such students and contribute to that growth. I have lived in Chicago for nearly seven years now: teaching at St Ignatius would allow me to grow even deeper roots in the community and the local Church, contributing in faith and participating in the greater good concretely, receiving greater faith and joy in return.

## Essay 2 — What excites you about our Catholic, Jesuit mission? How will you embrace and implement it?
> As a scientist and person of faith, I am excited to extend to others my personal integration of these two areas. The Jesuits have a long tradition of rigorous scholarship and missionary zeal; I would feel at home forming young minds to complement faith and humility with intellectual rigor, attending to each student individually and allowing them to challenge me as well. I have experience in faith formation as an OCIA instructor at Calvert House and Ignatian spirituality has played a role in my own development: I regularly practice Ignatian Gospel Contemplation via recorded prayers from Father Timothy Gallagher.
>
> In addition to modeling an integrated faith and intellectual life for the students, I hope to bring some of my knowledge and expertise in today’s most cutting edge research in physics. I have found that many concepts learned in introductory physics carry into research: I would be happy to bring in various friends who are research physicists to give accessible lectures to students—exposing them to the frontier of modern knowledge and open questions.
