## Submitted answers (2026-07-10, verbatim)

**Q1 — What pulls you toward questions about how science actually gets done?**

Having recently finished my PhD in physics at the University of Chicago, I am keenly aware of the human elements undergirding the scientific enterprise. Writing scientific articles is an exercise in understanding what the scientific community (or the tiny sliver of it one is targeting) actually cares about.

This was, in fact, a big shift in my approach to science. I began grad school thinking I should pursue, first and foremost, what I am interested in; I realize now I must first understand what everyone else cares about if I can hope to be listened to, or contribute at all. I do not think this a bad thing. I have come to enjoy the communal nature of scientific research---learning to navigate the landscape of varying agendas/motives is as interesting to me as any scientific question.

**Q2 — A moment when human factors shaped what got discovered (or didn't)**

I am currently looking for work in the field of AI safety research. My PhD work focused on statistical physics (how things are more than the sum of their parts; also known as emergence) and I am looking to apply that mathematical/theoretical apparatus to ensure reliable oversight of modern AI systems.

Applying to AI Safety Research Fellowships in the past few months, and engaging with this community's research more broadly, I have witnessed a tendency to eschew research directions concerned with fundamental understanding of LLMs and AI. Currently, there seems to be more interest in behavioral studies of LLMs (e.g. in what contexts what an LLM blackmail an individual? or when will it self-report its reasoning faithfully and reliably?).

There seems to be an outsized influence by one particular movement of individuals, the self-described rationalists, whose main oracle is the autodidact and prominent blogger, Eliezer Yudkowsky. This community seems to think, among many things, that super-intelligence is at hand, we have no guarantee that such an intelligence would not seek to harm or kill all of us, and therefore, research to AI alignment must take the straightest/shortest paths to ensure control. Behavioral research and interventions (which take for granted the notion that AI is goal-oriented as humans are) has gotten more results to this end, and fundamental research ought be deprioritized accordingly. This seems to be the case with research directions at frontier AI labs/companies.

In short, it seems as though a large swath of AI research is guided by fear, fueled by hypothetical doomsday scenarios---scenarios that take anthropomorphizing these systems for granted.

**Q3 — What is your most mimetic belief?**

I am a New York Jets fan. I hold onto the belief that they may someday win the superbowl, and furthermore, that continuing my fandom might someday lead to happiness. The Jets have been a terrible team for most of my life. They are known to be a horribly mismanaged organization. It appears both these facts will remain true for the foreseeable future. I am only a Jets fan because my family, especially my dad, is so attached to them.

I have sincerely tried to change the football team I root for, especially in college. I have found this to be impossible.
