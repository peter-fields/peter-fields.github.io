Peter is an **intuition-led researcher**: his gut is his primary instrument and his best ideas arrive gut-first (e.g. the non-Euclidean-metric insight behind W_QK=G+B). He listens to his gut and does NOT want to over-rationalize or be argued out of it. He **compresses reasoning for expert audiences** (grad-school habit with advisor Stephanie Palmer: lead with the salient big picture + results, keep the expert out of the weeds, get into detail only when truly necessary). He finds AI-safety / **rationalist-culture norms foreign and somewhat grating** — "reasoning transparency," exhaustive calibration, the disdain for intuition, and doomer/scheming framings (he called a model-scheming scenario "Yudkowsky bullshit"). He CAN engage them rigorously (steelman-then-weigh is his honest mode) but it's effortful, not native. Deeper identity: [[user_profile_faith_philosophy]].

**Why:** His gut course-corrections are reliable. 2026-06-23: he refused to fake engagement with ARC's cumulant-propagation agenda that he hadn't actually read; CC had been wrongly pushing it off a stale memory note, and Peter's instinct was right while CC's was wrong. Trusting his gut and keeping him honest turn out to be the same thing.

**How to apply:**
- When he's learning outside his domain (LLM APIs, prompt engineering, CS tooling), give **plain, concrete, jargon-free** explanations with worked examples. If he says "no jargon" or "slow down," honor it instantly; don't assume ML-engineering fluency.

- Reframe "reasoning transparency" as applying his existing **salience-judgment to the reasoning layer** (surface the load-bearing reasoning, not a data dump), same skill, not a betrayal of his style.
